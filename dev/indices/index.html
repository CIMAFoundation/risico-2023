    Fire Danger Indices¶

    Besides RISICO, risico-2023 computes the Canadian Fire Weather Index System and eight more fire danger indices used in different countries. They all read the same weather inputs, so one run can compare them on the same domain. The interactive demo runs all of them on one cell, side by side, and References lists the publication behind each.

    Each model is one entry of the configuration's models list; its type selects the model. The variable names in the tables are the internal_names an output lists (see Configuration).

    RISICO¶

    type: RISICO. Developed by CIMA Research Foundation and used operationally by the Italian Civil Protection. RISICO follows the moisture of the dead fine fuel hour by hour, drying it with temperature, humidity and wind and wetting it with rain, and turns it into the rate of spread and the intensity a fire would have in the vegetation of each cell, corrected for wind and slope (see How RISICO Works). The model is described by Fiorucci et al. (2008) and, in its current version, by Perello et al. (2025): see References.

    Model versions (model_version):

    • v2025: the current formulation: the moisture equations of v2023, with a revised rate of spread and weather effect;
    • v2023: revised dead fuel moisture equations and rate of spread;
    • legacy: the original equations. An unknown version also selects them.

    Static data: domain mask, slope, aspect, vegetation id and catalogue, and optionally the summer and winter PPF layers. Warm state: yes.

    dffm Dead fine fuel moisture %
    V Rate of spread m/h
    I Fireline intensity kW/m
    W Wind effect on spread (multiplier) –
    contrT Temperature effect on spread (multiplier) –
    VPPF, IPPF Spread and intensity weighted by the seasonal PPF layer m/h, kW/m

    The weather the model actually used can be written too: temperature (°C), humidity (%), windSpeed (m/s), windDir, rain (mm) and snowCover (cm).

    Canadian Fire Weather Index System¶

    type: FWI. The Canadian Forest Fire Weather Index System (Van Wagner, 1987), the most widely used fire danger rating system in the world. Three moisture codes track the drying of fuel layers of increasing depth, and three indices combine them with wind into a rating of fire behaviour. FWI is a daily index, computed from the weather at noon. See References.

    Model versions (model_version):

    • cffdrs: the standard daily system, with the equations and constants of the cffdrs R package and tested against its published values. It steps at each cell's local standard noon (time zone from the cell's position, no daylight saving time), with the noon weather interpolated from the hourly inputs and the rain accumulated since the previous noon.
    • legacy: the formulation of the Italian operational chain: noon in local civil time, daily step computed when the output is written.
    • sliding_window: computed on rolling 24-hour windows instead of at noon.

    Static data: a domain mask. Warm state: yes. Write one map per day with output_time_resolution: 24; with cffdrs, set output_time_offset to the hours from the run date at which every cell of the domain has passed its noon (for example 12 for a domain east of Greenwich).

    Variable Meaning
    ffmc Fine Fuel Moisture Code: moisture of litter and fine fuel
    dmc Duff Moisture Code: moisture of loosely compacted organic layers
    dc Drought Code: moisture of deep, compact organic layers
    isi Initial Spread Index: expected rate of spread
    bui Build-Up Index: total fuel available
    fwi Fire Weather Index: overall fire intensity rating
    ifwi IFWI, a non-linear rescaling of fwi
